Clean accuracy gives an incomplete account of a land-cover classifier's sensitivity to image changes. Semantic Analysis of Counterfactuals: Quality Under Transformations and Adaptive Benchmarking (SAC-QUTAB) evaluates ResNet-50 and ViT-Small/16 on paired EuroSAT RGB images, using three training seeds per model and separate training, validation, calibration, and final-test partitions. RGB gains, Fourier amplitude mixing, and resolution reduction are tested at three severities. Identity shams check the processing paths, and different-class controls provide a reference for representation displacement. A frozen protocol fixes the six checkpoints and evaluation inputs before final testing. ResNet achieves higher mean clean accuracy (98.63% versus 96.77%), whereas ViT retains more accuracy under severe resolution reduction (43.00% versus 12.94%). The model ordering therefore depends on the transformation family. Representation displacement, expressed as a percentile of a validation-control distribution, captures changes that confidence alone does not describe. Among model-specific clean-correct, non-sham pairs, adding this feature to transformed uncertainty and entropy improves failure-detection average precision in every run. Mean paired gains are 0.432 for ResNet and 0.402 for ViT. Source-group bootstrap intervals and variation across training seeds are reported separately. No human semantic-preservation review was conducted; the results measure sensitivity relative to the original dataset labels. Differences in pretraining recipes and hardware, together with limited seed and domain coverage, restrict conclusions about architecture. The project repository is publicly available for reproducibility [@repository].
